This Verilog module, `fpu_division_postnormalizer`, performs the post-normalization stage for floating-point division results, ensuring the output is correctly normalized, rounded, and flagged for any inaccuracies.

### Primary Function:
The module's primary function is to process the quotient and remainder from a floating-point division operation, normalize the result, apply the appropriate rounding, handle special cases (like zero, infinity, and NaN), and produce the final floating-point output while indicating if the result is inexact.

### Key Control Flow and Decision Points:
1. **Clocking and Input Latching**: The module captures all input signals on the rising edge of the clock, ensuring synchronized processing.
2. **Special Case Handling**: It identifies special cases like zero, infinity, and NaN by checking the exponent and significand fields of the operands.
3. **Normalization**: It adjusts for the normalization of the quotient, handling shifts left or right based on the quotient's leading zeros.
4. **Rounding Decision**: Based on the rounding mode, the module determines if rounding up is necessary by evaluating the guard, round, and sticky bits.

### Important Data Processing and Signal Interactions:
1. **Input Processing**: Inputs are latched into respective registers for subsequent stages. The exponents of the operands are extracted and checked for denormalization.
2. **Normalization**: The module shifts the quotient appropriately, considering leading zeros and the exponent adjustment, to achieve a normalized form.
3. **Exponent Calculation**: The normalized quotient's shift amount affects the final exponent calculation, which is adjusted based on the rounding result.
4. **Rounding**: The guard, round, and sticky bits are evaluated to decide if the fraction part should be incremented (rounded up).
5. **Output Assignment**: The final floating-point result is constructed from the sign bit, adjusted exponent, and rounded fraction, accounting for special cases like zero, infinity, and NaN.
6. **Inexact Flag**: The module sets the inexact flag if the result is inexact, due to non-zero remainder, lost bits, or a guard/round/sticky condition indicating a rounding adjustment.
